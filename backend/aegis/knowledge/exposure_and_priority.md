## Exposure versus impact
People, buildings and facilities inside a detected hazard extent are potentially exposed. Exposure is not impact: being inside a flood extent does not mean a person is displaced, injured or in need. Reports must use the terms "potentially exposed" or "potentially affected" unless ground information confirms impact.

## Population data
Gridded population products redistribute census counts to grid cells. The High Resolution Settlement Layer (Meta and CIESIN, about 30 m) and WorldPop (100 m) are widely used; both are model-based estimates whose vintage (often 2015-2020) may not reflect current population or seasonal movement. Totals from different products can differ substantially.

## Infrastructure data
OpenStreetMap is the most complete open global source of facilities and roads, but completeness varies strongly between regions. Absence of a mapped hospital or school does not mean none exists.

## Risk formulation
Disaster risk is commonly expressed as a combination of hazard, exposure and vulnerability (UNDRR terminology; the INFORM Risk Index of the European Commission Joint Research Centre). A multiplicative combination ensures that a cell with intense hazard but no exposure, or high exposure but no hazard, is not ranked as a top priority. Vulnerability and coping capacity are rarely available at fine resolution and should be reported as a gap.

## Life safety first
Humanitarian prioritisation places life-saving needs first. Hospitals, emergency services and shelters inside an affected area warrant priority verification because their loss reduces response capacity for everyone around them.

## Decision support, not decisions
Satellite-derived priorities support, but do not replace, the judgement of authorised responders. They should be verified with local information before resources are committed, and uncertain high-priority areas should be verified rather than ignored.
